Trapped

Life casts no shadows for you to stand in. The white light is so bright it nearly blinds you. It demands open eyes and a steady march forward, filling the heated air with a friction that burns your skin. You are told to thrive within that glare, to turn your scorched skin into charcoal just to keep your heart alive. But when the angle shifts and the air cools, you find refuge by the shore, watching the friction cease. Rows of bodies stand motionless, carrying scars that never seem to heal. You hope the shifting hue will soothe the wound. And when the moment comes, the relief is audible. The fading warmth is just enough to keep you there. You know you should walk away, yet you remain until darkness takes over. But the colors stay trapped inside your head, and the darkness feels brighter than the light. With the glare gone and your eyes finally open, you see there were never any scars on you at all.
